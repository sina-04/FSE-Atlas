# Food Science and Engineering — Website Product Specification

## 1. Project Overview

### Project Purpose

Develop a comprehensive academic, professional, and international opportunity platform specifically designed for **Food Science and Engineering (FSE)** students and graduates.

The platform should help students understand their undergraduate education, develop relevant technical competencies, explore careers in the food industry, discover international research opportunities, evaluate postgraduate programs, and plan educational or employment pathways abroad.

The website should serve as a centralized resource connecting academic education with real-world opportunities in food science, food technology, and food engineering.

### Target Audience

- Undergraduate Food Science and Engineering students.
- Recent Food Science and Engineering graduates.
- Students seeking internships or employment in the food industry.
- Students preparing for master's or PhD studies.
- Students interested in food-related scientific research.
- Graduates seeking international educational or employment opportunities.

### Core Objectives

1. Explain undergraduate courses and their practical relevance.
2. Connect academic subjects to food industry careers and research areas.
3. Recommend educational resources and technical skills.
4. Provide structured roadmaps for different professional roles.
5. Introduce graduate specializations and global research opportunities.
6. Explain international education and employment pathways.

# 3. Section Specifications

## 3.1. Undergraduate Courses

### Objective

Help Food Science and Engineering students understand their undergraduate curriculum, identify valuable educational resources, and evaluate how each course contributes to scientific research or industrial employment.

The University of Tehran's Food Science and Engineering undergraduate program should serve as the initial curriculum reference.

The architecture must eventually support additional universities and curriculum versions.

### 3.1.1. University of Tehran Curriculum Updates

**Description**

Develop a structured, navigable representation of the official undergraduate Food Science and Engineering curriculum.

Students should be able to examine their courses, understand prerequisite relationships, and identify how different subjects contribute to their academic development.

**Suggested Course Categories**

- Foundational Sciences: Mathematics, Physics, General Chemistry, Organic Chemistry, Biology, and Statistics.
- Core Food Sciences: Food Chemistry, Food Microbiology, Food Biochemistry, Food Analysis, and Food Nutrition fundamentals.
- Food Engineering: Food Process Engineering, Heat and Mass Transfer, Unit Operations, and Food Processing Equipment.
- Food Technology: Food Preservation, Food Packaging, Dairy Technology, Cereal Technology, Meat Processing, and related applications.
- Food Safety and Quality: Food Safety, Food Hygiene, Quality Control, and Quality Management.
- Practical Education: Laboratories, Industrial Internships, Research Projects, and Undergraduate Thesis or Final Project, where applicable.

These categories and courses are illustrative and must not be represented as the verified University of Tehran curriculum.

**Core Features**

- Display the complete verified curriculum.
- Categorize courses by academic group.
- Display course codes, titles, and credit units.
- Show prerequisites and corequisites.
- Organize courses by suggested academic semester.
- Visualize course prerequisite relationships.
- Display curriculum year and version.
- Identify officially documented curriculum revisions.
- Provide original university documents and reference links.

**Individual Course Pages**

Each course should have a dedicated page containing:

- Course title and description.
- Academic category.
- Credit units.
- Prerequisite courses.
- Main learning topics.
- Laboratory or practical components, if applicable.
- Supplementary learning resources.
- Relevant technical skills.
- Research applications.
- Industrial applications.
- Related careers and graduate specializations.

**Important**

The website must not invent official curriculum data or describe unverified documents as the latest version.

**Resource Categories**

- University lecture notes.

- Scientific software tutorials.

**Examples**

- Food microbiology textbooks.
- Microbial growth and inactivation resources.

- Open learning materials on fermentation.

For Food Process Engineering:

- Heat and mass transfer references.
- Food engineering textbooks.

- Drying and evaporation case studies.

**Core Features**

- Filter by resource format.

**Description**

Explain how undergraduate courses contribute to different research fields and food industry occupations.

Each course should be assessed separately across two dimensions.

#### A. Academic Research Relevance

Identify which scientific research areas depend on the concepts and methodologies taught in a particular course.

Examples:

**Food Chemistry**

Relevant to:

- Food ingredient interactions.
- Lipid oxidation.
- Food composition.
- Food stability.
- Functional food research.

**Food Microbiology**

Relevant to:

- Foodborne pathogens.
- Fermentation.
- Microbial food safety.
- Food spoilage.
- Food biotechnology.

**Food Process Engineering**

Relevant to:

- Process optimization.
- Thermal processing.
- Drying technologies.
- Nonthermal processing.
- Computational modeling of food processes.

#### B. Food Industry Relevance

Explain the importance of each course for various industrial roles.

Examples:

| Undergraduate Course     | Example Industry Applications                                   |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Food Chemistry           | Product formulation, ingredient analysis, product stability     |
| Food Microbiology        | Microbiological testing, food safety, fermentation              |
| Food Process Engineering | Production processes, equipment selection, process improvement  |
| Food Analysis            | Laboratory testing, compositional verification                  |
| Food Packaging           | Packaging development, shelf-life protection                    |

**Core Features**

- Display research relevance separately from industry relevance.
- Link each course to relevant job profiles.
- Link each course to relevant research areas.
- Provide concise explanations supporting the assessments.
- Allow comparisons between courses.
- Support evidence-based relevance indicators.

Any numerical relevance score must use a consistent, documented assessment method.

The platform must support relationships such as:

- Courses to skills.
- Courses to careers.
- Courses to research areas.
- Skills to careers.
- Skills to research areas.
- Careers to graduate programs.
- Research areas to professors.
- Research areas to research positions.
- Graduate programs to universities.
- Universities to countries.
- Countries to international pathways.

### Content Metadata

Each content entity should support appropriate fields for:

- Unique ID.
- Title.
- URL slug.
- Description.
- Category.
- Source URLs.
- Last verification date.
- Related entities.
- Publication status.
- Source classification.

Time-sensitive entries should additionally record publication dates, deadlines, validity periods, or status changes where relevant.

### Data Integrity

Distinguish between:

- Official institutional data.
- Peer-reviewed scientific evidence.
- Professional or regulatory standards.
- Third-party educational resources.
- Editorial descriptions and assessments.

Do not present editorial recommendations as official academic or regulatory requirements.

- Food laboratories.
- Industrial food production.
- Microscopy and microbiology.

# 9. Development Principles

1. **Food Science Focus:** All primary content and examples should be relevant to Food Science and Engineering students.
2. **Academic Accuracy:** Use verified curricula, scientific references, and official institutional information.
3. **Industry Relevance:** Connect academic learning to practical food manufacturing, food safety, laboratory, engineering, and research applications.
4. **Structured Data:** Maintain reusable entities rather than constructing disconnected static pages.
5. **Interconnected Content:** Enable natural navigation between courses, skills, careers, research, and international opportunities.
6. **Source Transparency:** Provide traceable information sources and verification dates.
7. **Scalability:** Allow expansion to additional universities, programs, research fields, and countries.
8. **Maintainability:** Keep content, data relationships, and presentation components logically separated.
9. **Realistic Guidance:** Distinguish undergraduate-accessible positions from roles requiring advanced education, experience, certifications, or legal authorization.
10. **Progressive Implementation:** Prioritize a functional, evidence-based foundation before introducing complex recommendation systems or automated data collection.

# 10. Final Product Vision

Build a centralized **Food Science and Engineering Academic, Career, Research, and International Opportunities Navigator**.

The website should help students answer six fundamental questions:

1. **What am I studying?** — Understand the undergraduate curriculum and individual courses.
2. **Why does it matter?** — Discover how academic knowledge applies to scientific research and the food industry.
3. **What skills should I develop?** — Identify laboratory, engineering, computational, regulatory, and professional competencies.
4. **What can I become?** — Explore food industry careers and understand the qualifications required for each role.
5. **What can I research or study next?** — Discover scientific research areas, professors, graduate programs, and academic opportunities.
6. **How can I pursue opportunities internationally?** — Understand relevant education, research, and employment pathways abroad.

The final product should function as an interconnected, evidence-based educational and professional guidance system rather than a conventional informational website.
